Decisions worth your yes or no

  • White card, colour on the edge. Today the whole card is the ISIC colour and the text is white on it — which is why it can hold two lines and no figures. The canvas's answer is a white card with a 5 px colour bar; that frees the surface for numbers and keeps the section readable at a glance.
  • Two segments in one bar (branch share, and the direct part inside it) instead of two bars. One row, one question: is this a hotspot or a doorway?
  • Three figures, and which three. Cumulative, direct, amount — the same three a reviewer asks for. Share of parent stays in the hover panel; it is a comparison, not a property of the node.
  • No mark for your own processes beyond the words. The meta line says “Model process”; the green left edge that first stood there means “carries a share of the result” on the canvas, and one sign with two meanings is worse than none.
  • No dashed outlines. A folded card is not drawn with a broken line; the chevron and the number beside it say what is hidden, and they say how much, which a dashed border cannot.
  • The zone keeps its two meanings (card = flows panel, zone = fold) and now shows how many children are hidden, which the chevron alone never said.
  • The compact rung keeps the same reading order — value on the left, share bold on the right — so the eye does not have to relearn the card halfway down the tree.
  • Two sizes. Full card for the first three tiers, compact from the fourth — a full card on a 23-child tier reads as a wall. If you would rather have one size everywhere, say so and it goes.
  • Wider than the canvas node (272 px against 210): three columns of figures need it. The tier gap grows with it, so a deep tree gets wider — the fit-to-view button already handles that.
