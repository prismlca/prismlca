Comment on the three ISO 14044 §4.5 checks below the tables. The tables state the findings; this paragraph states what they mean, which is what the standard asks for at this point.
- Completeness: what the structural check covers and what it does not. It verifies reference products, links and signs — it cannot tell whether a life cycle stage is missing, so say which conclusions the data supports and which it does not.
- Sensitivity: whether the study's statements survive the spread across the variants. A wide spread on an indicator means the conclusion there depends on the assumption that was varied; a narrow one means it does not.
- Parameter sensitivity, only where `checks.tornado` is in your material (the tornado figures under the sensitivity check): which single parameters move each indicator most, and what follows for the data behind them — the parameter with the longest bar is the figure whose source matters most. Name every parameter you mention, and its effect, by placeholder ({{checks.tornado.0.parameters.0.name}}: {{checks.tornado.0.parameters.0.low_text}} / {{checks.tornado.0.parameters.0.high_text}}) — never typed out: the parameters are a ranking, and only a placeholder lets the report notice when a recompute reorders them. Say what was done: one parameter at a time by {{checks.tornado.0.delta_text}}, on the baseline of the reference process — not an uncertainty analysis, and nothing about parameters moving together. A parameter marked `limited` (* in the figure) stopped at its allowed values, so its bar is shorter than the range; one marked `breaks` (!) pushed another parameter outside its allowed values, so its bar describes a model the rules exclude — mention that where you name such a parameter, and never build a conclusion on it. Without `checks.tornado`, write nothing about a tornado or a parameter sensitivity.
- Consistency: that this is the mechanically checkable part — same indicators, one background database, one functional unit. Whether the modelling itself was consistent in depth and data years is a judgement, and if you cannot support it, say that it was not assessed.
**Then the data quality, along the axes ISO 14044 names — this is where a screening study earns or loses its credibility.** Eight published product LCAs were read for this (2026-08-27) and the strongest gives its interpretation chapter a section per axis. One sentence each, from what you were given and nothing else:
  · **Completeness** — which parts of the system rest on documented figures and which on estimates. The parameter comments say which is which.
  · **Representativeness** — whether the datasets match the study's geography and technology. A GLO market standing in for a German supply chain is the sentence here; so is a proxy from another technology. The `exchange_notes` record where an input was chosen as a proxy, and why.
  · **Consistency** — one background database, one functional unit, one method family across every variant. If any of the three is mixed, that is the finding.
  · **Reproducibility** — whether somebody holding this report could rebuild the model: which background version, which method version, which assumptions are written down.
  Say plainly where an axis cannot be judged from what you have — “the reference years of the background datasets are not recorded here” beats a guess. **Never invent a data-quality score or a pedigree rating**; none was computed.
  **An axis says what your own paragraphs above have not already said.** Completeness and consistency are the two the mechanical checks cover, and your first paragraphs report those checks — so do not state their result twice. Point back to it in a clause and spend the sentence on the part no check can reach: the completeness check verifies the wiring, not whether a stage was left out of the scope; the consistency check counts databases and units, not whether the modelling is consistent in depth. That judgement is the axis. Read as one chapter, this section adds four judgements — it does not repeat two findings.
**Optional, where the report has the evidence:** name the band the study spans — the lowest and highest computed variant with the assumption that separates them. Real reports carry this as a best/worst case, and it is the number a reader remembers.
