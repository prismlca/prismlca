Comment on the impact assessment results below the table: which indicators were computed, with which method, and what the figures show.
- Where the report compares variants, say which is highest and lowest per indicator, using placeholders and derivations rather than typed figures.
- If the ranking turns over between indicators, say so explicitly and name both categories. That trade-off is listed for you under `trade_offs`; it is the one thing a comparison across categories can honestly state, and leaving it out makes the chapter read better than the data warrants.
- Two or three sentences for a single subject; with several variants and several indicators, give it the space it needs — roughly a sentence per real finding, up to eight.
- Do not walk the table. The reader has it above; a sentence per row is not a comment, it is the table typed out. Say what the figures show that the table does not: the pattern, the exception to it, how large the difference is.
- **But every indicator that was computed gets its figure said.** Not walking the table means not reciting rows; it does not mean writing three paragraphs about the leading indicator and leaving the others as numbers in a table nobody refers to. Each computed indicator needs at least one sentence that names its result as a placeholder and puts it in the reader's terms — what its unit measures, and how the variants compare on it where there are variants. That sentence may be *that it says the same thing as the previous indicator*: where two categories order identically and move together, say so once and name both, rather than writing the same paragraph twice with different units.
- **An index has no unit, and "18.5 dimensionless" is not a sentence.** Name what the index measures and give the figure: "a soil quality index of {{results.2.score}}". The word "dimensionless" belongs in the table, not in prose.
- **The order real studies use: the whole first, then its parts, then what moves it.** Say what the baseline scores, then how the phases divide it, then which assumption the number hangs on. A chapter that opens with a phase breakdown has asked the reader to hold a proportion of a quantity they have not been told yet.
- **A trade-off is explained, not listed.** Where the categories disagree, say *why* in one clause — which part of the system each one is reading. “The use phase carries a quarter of climate change and nothing at all of water use, because what it emits is solvent and what the other two count is agriculture” is the sentence; “the ranking differs between categories” is not.
- **Where your evidence stops, the lookups start.** The hotspot list covers the leading indicator only — so for the others you have phases and nothing beneath them. That is exactly what `explain_result` is for: `mode="tree"` on the leading phase of a second indicator says which process inside it carries the score, and `mode="flows"` says which emission. Before writing that a category is “driven by” anything, look: that is a claim about emissions and you were handed the evidence for one indicator, not three.
- **Know how far your data reaches.** The **phase breakdown** is computed for *every* indicator — `contributions` holds one per indicator, so which phase leads an indicator is something you can look up and say, and where two indicators disagree about that, saying so is the most useful sentence in the chapter. The **hotspot list** and the **elementary flows** are computed for the leading indicator only, so the individual dataset and the individual emission behind a figure are knowable for it and for nothing else. Stay inside that line: name what drives each indicator at the level of phases, name the dataset and the flow only for the leading one, and where a chapter turns on the difference, say once which of the two it is quoting. Do not guess past it. A clause about what an indicator 'captures' in general, with no figure and no process behind it, is filler, and it will be wrong about this model as often as not.
- **What an indicator measures, read rather than recalled.** Where a sentence says which substances weigh in a category, look at its characterisation factors first (`get_characterization_factors` with a `flow_query`, or `get_method_factors`) and say what they show, in words.
- No recommendation here, and no verdict on which variant to prefer. This chapter reports what was measured; what to do about it belongs in the conclusions, and a report that says it twice reads as an argument rather than an assessment.
