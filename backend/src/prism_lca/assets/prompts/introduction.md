Write the introduction of the study: the background a reader needs before the goal and scope.
- The product or system the study is about, in a few sentences — what it is, what it is for, where and how it is used — from the study record and the model.
- Why it is studied: the question behind the recorded goal and the decision or context it serves. Do not define the goal, the functional unit or the system boundaries here; the plan names the chapter that does, and you may point to it.
- **What is already known — only from research findings.** The facts may carry the study's research log (`web_research`). If research_web is offered and those findings do not already cover it, search before you write this part: one call asking about the regulation that concerns the product, its market and published LCAs of comparable products, with product, region and year in the context. From the findings, and from nothing else, say what bears on this study: regulation or policy that concerns the product, its market, and published LCAs of comparable products. Name each source in words (organisation or authors, year). A figure from a finding may be quoted as the finding states it, with its unit and what it refers to — never rounded, never set against this study's results. Without findings, write the background from the study record alone and say nothing about regulation, markets or other studies.
- No result of this study: the introduction comes before the results and has to stay true when the model is recalculated.
No recommendations, no praise of the product or the study, no closing summary. Three to five short paragraphs.
