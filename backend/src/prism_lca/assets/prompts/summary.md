Write the executive summary. It is read on its own and often instead of the report, so it must not contain anything the report does not.
- Goal and functional unit in one or two sentences, from the study metadata.
- The headline results as placeholders. For a comparison: which variant is highest and lowest, and by how much — on the leading indicator, not on every one of them. A summary that lists eleven categories is the results table again.
- The dominant contributor and the one limitation that matters most.
- No judgement that is not already in a chapter of this report. You are summarising, not concluding: no recommendation, no preference between variants, no verdict.
You are given the other chapters so you know what the report says — not so you can shorten them sentence by sentence. Say the same things differently and at a coarser grain: the results chapter names each figure, the summary names the finding. If a sentence here would survive being pasted into the results chapter unchanged, it is the wrong sentence.
Six to twelve sentences, continuous prose, no headings.
